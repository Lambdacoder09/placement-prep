# Chapter 8 — Time & Work

*Turn days into rates, then just add.*

## What you need to know

**WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW**

**The one idea: work per day**
If a worker finishes a job in $a$ days, then in one day that worker does $1/a$ of the job.
$ "work done" = "rate" \times "time" $

**Combining workers** (add the rates, never the days)
$ 1/a + 1/b = (a+b)/(a b) \quad \\Rightarrow \quad "together they take" \quad (a b)/(a + b) " days" $
Three workers: $\quad (a b c)/(a b + b c + c a)$ days.

**The LCM method** (faster in an exam)
Let total work $=$ LCM of the given days. Then each worker's daily output is a whole number.
Example: $a = 12$, $b = 18$. Total $= 36$ units. A does 3 units/day, B does 2 units/day.

**Efficiency**
Efficiency is proportional to $1/"time"$.
Times in ratio $a : b$ $\\Rightarrow$ efficiencies in ratio $b : a$.
"A is $p$ percent more efficient than B" $\\Rightarrow$ A's time $= $ B's time $\div (1 + p/100)$.

**The work equation**
$ (M_1 D_1 H_1)/W_1 = (M_2 D_2 H_2)/W_2 $
$M$ men, $D$ days, $H$ hours per day, $W$ amount of work.

**Wages**
Pay is split in the ratio of **work actually done**, which for equal working time is the ratio of efficiencies.

**Pipes and cisterns**
Filling pipe: rate is positive. Emptying pipe or leak: rate is negative.
$ "net rate" = sum "filling rates" - sum "emptying rates" $
Tank fills in $1 \div$ (net rate). A negative net rate means the tank never fills.

**Leak from two times**
Pipe alone fills in $a$ h; with a leak it takes $b$ h ($b > a$):
$ "leak rate" = 1/a - 1/b \quad \\Rightarrow \quad "leak alone empties in" \quad (a b)/(b - a) " hours" $

**Alternate days**
Work out the amount finished in one full 2-day block, see how many whole blocks fit, then finish the remainder day by day. The last day is usually a fraction.

**Three pair-totals**
If the pairs take $p$, $q$ and $r$ days (A and B in $p$, B and C in $q$, C and A in $r$):
$ 1/p + 1/q + 1/r = 2 \times ("one day's work of" A + B + C) $
So the rate of all three together is half that sum, and each single rate is one subtraction away.

## Warm-up

### Warm-up

**Example 1**

A can finish a job in 12 days. How much does he finish in one day?

**Solution**

One day's work $= 1/12$ of the job.

**➜ Answer: $1/12$ of the job**

**Example 2**

A does a job in 10 days and B in 15 days. How long do they take together?

**Solution**

Together in one day: $1/10 + 1/15$. LCM of 10 and 15 is 30.
$ 3/30 + 2/30 = 5/30 = 1/6 $
So they finish in 6 days.

**➜ Answer: 6 days**

**Example 3**

A takes 20 days and B takes 30 days. Find the time together.

**Solution**

$ 1/20 + 1/30 = 3/60 + 2/60 = 5/60 = 1/12 $
Time together $= 12$ days.

**➜ Answer: 12 days**

**Example 4**

A completes $1/5$ of a job in 4 days. How long does the whole job take him?

**Solution**

$1/5$ of the job takes 4 days, so the whole job takes $4 \times 5 = 20$ days.

**➜ Answer: 20 days**

**Example 5**

Pipe P fills a tank in 8 hours and pipe Q fills it in 12 hours. How long do both together take?

**Solution**

$ 1/8 + 1/12 = 3/24 + 2/24 = 5/24 $
Time $= 24/5 = 4.8$ hours.

**➜ Answer: 4.8 hours**

**Example 6**

A tap fills a tank in 6 hours. A leak can empty the full tank in 18 hours. With both working, how long does the tank take to fill?

**Solution**

Net rate $= 1/6 - 1/18 = 3/18 - 1/18 = 2/18 = 1/9$ per hour. \
Time $= 9$ hours.

**➜ Answer: 9 hours**

**Example 7**

A is twice as efficient as B. B alone takes 18 days. How long does A take?

**Solution**

Twice the efficiency means half the time: $18/2 = 9$ days.

**➜ Answer: 9 days**

**Example 8**

Ten men build a wall in 12 days. How long would one man take?

**Solution**

Total work $= 10 \times 12 = 120$ man-days. \
One man needs $120/1 = 120$ days.

**➜ Answer: 120 days**

## Tier 1 — Service companies

### Tier 1 — Service · TCS NQT, Accenture, Infosys, Wipro, Capgemini

**Example 9** `TCS NQT pattern`

A and B together finish a job in 12 days. A alone takes 20 days. How long does B alone take?

**Solution**

B's one-day work $=$ (together) $-$ (A alone).
$ 1/12 - 1/20 $
LCM of 12 and 20 is 60.
$ 5/60 - 3/60 = 2/60 = 1/30 $
So B alone takes 30 days.

**➜ Answer: 30 days**

---
⚠️ **TRAP**

Never write "together they take $12$, A takes $20$, so B takes $20 - 12 = 8$ days".
Days do not subtract. **Rates** subtract. Convert to $1/"days"$ first, every single time.

**Example 10** `Infosys pattern`

A can do a job in 12 days and B in 18 days. They work together for 4 days, then A leaves and B finishes the rest. Find the total number of days taken.

**Solution**

Use the LCM method. LCM of 12 and 18 is 36, so let the job be 36 units.

A does $36/12 = 3$ units per day. \
B does $36/18 = 2$ units per day. \
Together they do $3 + 2 = 5$ units per day.

In 4 days together: $5 \times 4 = 20$ units. \
Remaining work $= 36 - 20 = 16$ units.

B alone at 2 units per day needs $16/2 = 8$ days.

Total $= 4 + 8 = 12$ days.

**➜ Answer: 12 days**

---
💡 **SHORTCUT**

The LCM method kills fractions. Set total work $=$ LCM of the given day counts, and every rate becomes a whole number. You will finish these questions in half the time.

**Example 11** `Wipro pattern`

A, B and C can do a job alone in 10, 12 and 15 days. How long do all three together take?

**Solution**

LCM of 10, 12 and 15 is 60. Let the job be 60 units.

A: $60/10 = 6$ units per day. \
B: $60/12 = 5$ units per day. \
C: $60/15 = 4$ units per day.

Together: $6 + 5 + 4 = 15$ units per day.
$ "time" = 60/15 = 4 " days" $

**➜ Answer: 4 days**

**Example 12** `Accenture pattern`

A and B together take 12 days, B and C together take 15 days, and A and C together take 20 days. How long would each take alone, and how long would all three take together?

**Solution**

Add the three one-day works:
$ 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 $
LCM of 12, 15 and 20 is 60.
$ 5/60 + 4/60 + 3/60 = 12/60 = 1/5 $

But each of A, B and C has been counted twice in that sum, so
$ 2 \times (A + B + C) = 1/5 \quad \\Rightarrow \quad A + B + C = 1/10 $
All three together: **10 days**.

A alone $= (A+B+C) - (B+C) = 1/10 - 1/15 = 3/30 - 2/30 = 1/30$ $\\Rightarrow$ 30 days. \
B alone $= (A+B+C) - (A+C) = 1/10 - 1/20 = 2/20 - 1/20 = 1/20$ $\\Rightarrow$ 20 days. \
C alone $= (A+B+C) - (A+B) = 1/10 - 1/12 = 6/60 - 5/60 = 1/60$ $\\Rightarrow$ 60 days.

Check: $1/30 + 1/20 = 2/60 + 3/60 = 5/60 = 1/12$. Matches the given A and B figure.

**➜ Answer: All three 10 days; A 30, B 20, C 60 days**

---
💡 **SHORTCUT**

Given the three pair-totals, **add them and halve**. That single move gives the rate of all three together, and every individual rate is then one subtraction away.

**Example 13** `Capgemini pattern`

A is 50 percent more efficient than B. A alone finishes a job in 12 days. How long do A and B together take?

**Solution**

A's efficiency $= 1.5 \times$ B's efficiency, so A's time $= "B's time" \div 1.5$.
$ 12 = "B's time"/1.5 \quad \\Rightarrow \quad "B's time" = 12 \times 1.5 = 18 " days" $

Together:
$ 1/12 + 1/18 = 3/36 + 2/36 = 5/36 $
$ "time" = 36/5 = 7.2 " days" $

**➜ Answer: 7.2 days**

**Example 14** `Cognizant pattern`

A can do a job in 12 days and B in 15 days. They work together and are paid Rs.~5,400 in all. How should the money be split?

**Solution**

Pay follows work done, and both work the same number of days, so pay follows the rates.

Rate ratio $= 1/12 : 1/15$. Multiply both by 60:
$ 60/12 : 60/15 = 5 : 4 $

Total parts $= 5 + 4 = 9$. \
One part $= 5400/9 = 600$.

A gets $5 \times 600 = $ Rs.~3,000. \
B gets $4 \times 600 = $ Rs.~2,400. \
Check: $3000 + 2400 = 5400$. Correct.

**➜ Answer: A: Rs.~3,000, B: Rs.~2,400**

---
⚠️ **TRAP**

Do not split wages as $12 : 15$. The faster worker must earn **more**, not less.
Wages go in the ratio of one-day work, which is the **inverse** of the day counts.

**Example 15** `TCS NQT pattern`

Twelve workers, working 8 hours a day, build a wall in 15 days. How many days would 18 workers need, working 10 hours a day?

**Solution**

Total work in worker-hours:
$ 12 \times 8 \times 15 = 96 \times 15 = 1440 " worker-hours" $

New team does $18 \times 10 = 180$ worker-hours per day.
$ "days" = 1440/180 = 8 $

**➜ Answer: 8 days**

**Example 16** `Wipro pattern`

A pipe fills a tank in 10 hours. Because of a leak at the bottom it actually takes 15 hours. How long would the leak alone take to empty the full tank?

**Solution**

Let the leak empty the full tank in $L$ hours.
$ 1/10 - 1/L = 1/15 $
$ 1/L = 1/10 - 1/15 = 3/30 - 2/30 = 1/30 $
$ L = 30 " hours" $

**➜ Answer: 30 hours**

**Example 17** `Accenture pattern`

Pipes A and B fill a tank in 12 and 16 hours. Outlet pipe C empties the full tank in 24 hours. If all three are open, how long does the tank take to fill?

**Solution**

LCM of 12, 16 and 24 is 48. Let the tank be 48 units.

A: $48/12 = 4$ units per hour (in). \
B: $48/16 = 3$ units per hour (in). \
C: $48/24 = 2$ units per hour (out).

Net $= 4 + 3 - 2 = 5$ units per hour.
$ "time" = 48/5 = 9.6 " hours" $

**➜ Answer: 9.6 hours**

---
⚠️ **TRAP**

The outlet pipe gets a **minus** sign. Adding all three rates gives $48/9 \approx 5.3$ hours, a classic wrong option planted in these papers.

**Example 18** `Infosys pattern`

A can do a job in 9 days and B in 18 days. They work on alternate days with A starting. In how many days is the job finished?

**Solution**

LCM of 9 and 18 is 18. Let the job be 18 units. \
A does $18/9 = 2$ units per day. B does $18/18 = 1$ unit per day.

One 2-day block (A then B) $= 2 + 1 = 3$ units.

Five blocks $= 10$ days and $5 \times 3 = 15$ units. \
Remaining $= 18 - 15 = 3$ units.

Day 11 is A's turn: he does 2 units, total 17 units, 1 unit left. \
Day 12 is B's turn: he does exactly 1 unit, finishing the job.

Total $= 12$ days.

**➜ Answer: 12 days**

**Example 19** `Capgemini pattern`

Eighteen men can finish a job in 20 days. After 5 days, 3 men leave. How many more days does the job take?

**Solution**

Total work $= 18 \times 20 = 360$ man-days.

Done in 5 days $= 18 \times 5 = 90$ man-days. \
Remaining $= 360 - 90 = 270$ man-days.

Men left $= 18 - 3 = 15$.
$ "days needed" = 270/15 = 18 " days" $
Total elapsed $= 5 + 18 = 23$ days.

**➜ Answer: 18 more days (23 days in all)**

**Example 20** `TCS NQT pattern`

A contractor agrees to finish a road in 40 days and puts 30 men on it. After 25 days only half the road is done. How many extra men must he hire to finish on time?

**Solution**

Work done in 25 days $= 30 \times 25 = 750$ man-days, and that is half the road. \
So the whole road $= 2 \times 750 = 1500$ man-days.

Remaining work $= 750$ man-days. \
Days left $= 40 - 25 = 15$.
$ "men needed" = 750/15 = 50 $
He already has 30, so he must hire $50 - 30 = 20$ more.

**➜ Answer: 20 extra men**

## Tier 2 — Singapore & Thailand

### Tier 2 — Singapore & Thailand · Grab, Shopee, GIC, DBS, Agoda, SCB

**Example 21** `Grab · pattern`

A support team of 6 agents clears 480 tickets in 4 hours. All agents work at the same rate. How many agents are needed to clear 900 tickets in 5 hours?

**Solution**

Rate per agent per hour:
$ 480/(6 \times 4) = 480/24 = 20 " tickets" $

Required throughput:
$ 900/5 = 180 " tickets per hour" $

Agents needed:
$ 180/20 = 9 $

**➜ Answer: 9 agents**

**Example 22** `Shopee · pattern`

A team of 4 senior packers can pack one shipment in 9 hours. A team of 6 junior packers can pack the same shipment in 18 hours. How long will 2 seniors and 3 juniors take, working together?

**Solution**

Take the shipment as 1 unit of work.

4 seniors for 9 hours do the whole shipment:
$ 4 s \times 9 = 1 \quad \\Rightarrow \quad s = 1/36 " shipment per hour per senior" $

6 juniors for 18 hours do the whole shipment:
$ 6 j \times 18 = 1 \quad \\Rightarrow \quad j = 1/108 " shipment per hour per junior" $

Mixed team rate:
$ 2 s + 3 j = 2/36 + 3/108 = 6/108 + 3/108 = 9/108 = 1/12 $

Time $= 12$ hours.

**➜ Answer: 12 hours**

**Example 23** `DBS · pattern`

A migration project is estimated at 1,440 person-hours. A team of 8 people works 6 hours a day. After 10 days, 2 more people join the team on the same 6-hour day. How many days does the project take in all?

**Solution**

Team output before the joiners: $8 \times 6 = 48$ person-hours per day. \
Done in 10 days: $48 \times 10 = 480$ person-hours. \
Remaining: $1440 - 480 = 960$ person-hours.

New team output: $10 \times 6 = 60$ person-hours per day.
$ "days needed" = 960/60 = 16 $
Total $= 10 + 16 = 26$ days.

**➜ Answer: 26 days**

**Example 24** `GIC · pattern`

A 760 litre tank is empty. Inlet A is opened at the start and delivers 40 litres per minute. Inlet B, at 30 litres per minute, is opened 4 minutes later. A drain valve removing 25 litres per minute is opened 6 minutes after B. When is the tank full?

**Solution**

**Minute 0 to 4** — only A.
$ 40 \times 4 = 160 " litres" $

**Minute 4 to 10** — A and B, that is $40 + 30 = 70$ litres per minute for 6 minutes.
$ 70 \times 6 = 420 " litres" $
Running total $= 160 + 420 = 580$ litres.

**From minute 10** — A and B in, drain out: $40 + 30 - 25 = 45$ litres per minute. \
Still needed $= 760 - 580 = 180$ litres.
$ 180/45 = 4 " minutes" $

Tank is full at minute $10 + 4 = 14$.

**➜ Answer: 14 minutes after A was opened**

**Example 25** `Agoda · pattern`

A and B can complete a data migration alone in 20 and 30 days. They start together, but A is pulled onto another project 5 days before the migration is finished. How many days does the whole migration take?

**Solution**

Let the total time be $T$ days. \
B works for all $T$ days. A works for $T - 5$ days.
$ (T-5)/20 + T/30 = 1 $
Multiply everything by 60:
$ 3(T - 5) + 2 T = 60 $
$ 3T - 15 + 2T = 60 $
$ 5T = 75 \quad \\Rightarrow \quad T = 15 " days" $

Check: A works 10 days $\\Rightarrow 10/20 = 1/2$. B works 15 days $\\Rightarrow 15/30 = 1/2$. Sum $= 1$. Correct.

**➜ Answer: 15 days**

---
⚠️ **TRAP**

"A leaves 5 days before the end" does **not** mean A worked 5 days.
It means A worked $T - 5$ days, where $T$ is the answer you are still looking for.
Set up the equation in $T$ and let the algebra find it.

**Example 26** `SCB · pattern`

A, B and C together complete an audit in 10 days and are paid S$18,000. A alone would take 24 days and B alone 30 days. Find each person's share.

**Solution**

C's daily rate:
$ 1/10 - 1/24 - 1/30 $
LCM of 10, 24 and 30 is 120.
$ 12/120 - 5/120 - 4/120 = 3/120 = 1/40 $
So C alone would take 40 days.

All three work the same 10 days, so pay goes in the ratio of rates, that is
$ 5 : 4 : 3 \quad ("the numerators over 120") $

Total parts $= 12$, so one part $= 18000/12 = 1500$.

A: $5 \times 1500 = $ S$7,500. \
B: $4 times 1500 = $ S$6,000. \
C: $3 \times 1500 = $ S$4,500. \
Check: $7500 + 6000 + 4500 = 18000$. Correct.

**➜ Answer: A S$7,500, B S$6,000, C S$4,500**

---
💡 **SHORTCUT**

When you put all the rates over a **common** denominator, the numerators are already the wage ratio. No extra step is needed.

**Example 27** `LINE MAN · pattern`

A fleet of 15 riders was scheduled to deliver 1,800 orders in 6 days, every rider working at the same rate. At the end of day 3 the order book grew to 2,400 orders and 3 riders resigned. How many extra riders must be hired from day 4 so that all 2,400 orders still go out by the end of day 6?

**Solution**

Rate per rider per day:
$ 1800/(15 \times 6) = 1800/90 = 20 " orders" $

Delivered in the first 3 days:
$ 15 \times 3 \times 20 = 900 " orders" $

Still to deliver: $2400 - 900 = 1500$ orders in 3 days.
$ "orders per day needed" = 1500/3 = 500 $
$ "riders needed" = 500/20 = 25 $

Riders in hand from day 4 $= 15 - 3 = 12$. \
Extra riders $= 25 - 12 = 13$.

**➜ Answer: 13 extra riders**

**Example 28** `Razer · pattern`

Pipes P and Q can fill a tank in 6 hours and 8 hours. Both are opened together, but a leak at the bottom means the tank fills in 4 hours. How long would the leak alone take to empty the full tank?

**Solution**

Rate of P and Q together:
$ 1/6 + 1/8 = 4/24 + 3/24 = 7/24 $

With the leak, the observed rate is $1/4 = 6/24$.
$ "leak rate" = 7/24 - 6/24 = 1/24 $
The leak alone empties the full tank in 24 hours.

**➜ Answer: 24 hours**

**Example 29** `Sea Group · pattern`

A build task needs 24 working days. The team works 6 days a week with Sunday off, and starts on a Monday. On which calendar day of the project is the task finished?

**Solution**

Each calendar week gives 6 working days.

$24 \div 6 = 4$ full weeks of working days, with nothing left over.

Week 1: working days 1 to 6 fall on calendar days 1 to 6 (Mon to Sat). Calendar day 7 is Sunday, no work. \
Week 2: working days 7 to 12 on calendar days 8 to 13. \
Week 3: working days 13 to 18 on calendar days 15 to 20. \
Week 4: working days 19 to 24 on calendar days 22 to 27.

So working day 24 is calendar day 27, a Saturday.

**➜ Answer: Calendar day 27 (a Saturday)**

---
⚠️ **TRAP**

Do not answer 28 calendar days. Four weeks contain four Sundays, but the last Sunday comes **after** the job is already done, so it is not counted.

**Example 30** `Grab · pattern`

Engineer A finishes $3/5$ of a task in 9 days working alone. Engineer B then joins, and the two of them finish the remaining part in 4 days. How long would B alone have taken for the whole task?

**Solution**

A's daily rate:
$ (3/5)/9 = 3/45 = 1/15 $

Remaining work $= 1 - 3/5 = 2/5$, done by both in 4 days, so the combined rate is
$ (2/5)/4 = 2/20 = 1/10 $

B's rate:
$ 1/10 - 1/15 = 3/30 - 2/30 = 1/30 $
B alone would take 30 days.

**➜ Answer: 30 days**

+ **3 hours 45 minutes** — first half takes 3 hours; four taps give $4/6 = 2/3$ of a tank per hour, so the second half takes $(1/2) \div (2/3) = 3/4$ hour.
+ **A THB~4,800, B THB~3,600, C THB~6,000** — over 96 the rates are $12, 4, 3$ for all three, A and B, so C is $12 - 4 - 3 = 5$; ratio $4 : 3 : 5$ and one part is 1,200.

### Tier 3 — Product · Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Goldman Sachs, D.E. Shaw, Adobe

**Example 31** `Google · pattern`

A can finish a job in 6 days and B in 9 days. They work on alternate days. Show that it matters who starts, and find the total time in each case.

**Solution**

**Insight: full 2-day blocks are identical whoever starts. Only the ragged end differs, and the worker who starts gets one extra turn in that ragged end.**

LCM of 6 and 9 is 18, so let the job be 18 units. \
A does $18/6 = 3$ units per day. B does $18/9 = 2$ units per day. \
One 2-day block $= 3 + 2 = 5$ units.

**Case 1: A starts.** \
Three blocks $= 6$ days and $3 \times 5 = 15$ units. Remaining $= 3$ units. \
Day 7 is A's turn and A does exactly 3 units. Job done.
$ "total" = 7 " days" $

**Case 2: B starts.** \
Three blocks $= 6$ days and 15 units, exactly as before. Remaining $= 3$ units. \
Day 7 is B's turn: B does 2 units, leaving 1 unit. \
Day 8 is A's turn: A works at 3 units per day and needs only 1 unit, taking $1/3$ of a day.
$ "total" = 7 + 1/3 = 22/3 \approx 7.33 " days" $

So starting with the faster worker saves $1/3$ of a day.

**➜ Answer: A first: 7 days. B first: $22/3 \approx 7.33$ days**

---
💡 **SHORTCUT**

For alternate-day work, always put the **faster** worker first when you are asked for the minimum time. The blocks are identical; the faster worker simply eats more of the leftover.

**Example 32** `Goldman Sachs · pattern`

A ticket is assigned at random, with equal probability, to one of three engineers. Working alone they would need 6, 9 and 18 days.
(a) What is the expected number of days to close the ticket?
(b) If instead all three worked on it together, how long would it take?
(c) Explain the gap between the two answers.

**Solution**

**Insight: expectation averages the times, but teamwork averages the rates. Averaging rates and then inverting gives a much smaller number.**

**(a)** Each engineer is equally likely, so each has probability $1/3$.
$ E = 1/3 \times 6 + 1/3 \times 9 + 1/3 \times 18 = (6 + 9 + 18)/3 = 33/3 = 11 " days" $

**(b)** Add the rates. LCM of 6, 9 and 18 is 18.
$ 1/6 + 1/9 + 1/18 = 3/18 + 2/18 + 1/18 = 6/18 = 1/3 $
$ "time" = 3 " days" $

**(c)** Part (a) is the arithmetic mean of the three times, 11 days. \
Part (b) is one third of the harmonic mean of the three times:
$ "harmonic mean" = 3/(1/6 + 1/9 + 1/18) = 3/(1/3) = 9 " days" $
and three workers at that average rate finish in $9/3 = 3$ days.

The harmonic mean is never larger than the arithmetic mean, and the slow engineer drags the arithmetic mean up while barely affecting the combined rate. That is the whole gap.

**➜ Answer: (a) 11 days   (b) 3 days**

**Example 33** `D. E. Shaw · pattern`

Thirty workers can dig a canal in 10 days. To cut costs the contractor sends one worker home at the end of every day, starting with the end of day 1. In how many days is the canal finished?

**Solution**

**Insight: daily output is no longer constant, it is an arithmetic progression. Sum the progression and compare with the total work.**

Total work $= 30 \times 10 = 300$ worker-days.

Workers present on day $i$ $= 30 - (i - 1) = 31 - i$. \
Day 1: 30, day 2: 29, day 3: 28, and so on.

Work finished after $k$ days:
$ S_k = sum_(i=1)^k (31 - i) = 31 k - (k(k+1))/2 $

Try $k = 12$:
$ S_12 = 31 \times 12 - (12 \times 13)/2 = 372 - 78 = 294 $
That is short of 300, so the canal is not done on day 12.

Try $k = 13$:
$ S_13 = 31 \times 13 - (13 \times 14)/2 = 403 - 91 = 312 $
That overshoots 300, so the canal is finished during day 13.

Work left at the start of day 13 $= 300 - 294 = 6$ worker-days. \
Workers on day 13 $= 31 - 13 = 18$.
$ "fraction of day 13 needed" = 6/18 = 1/3 $

Total $= 12 + 1/3 = 37/3 \approx 12.33$ days.

**➜ Answer: $12 1/3$ days — it finishes one third of the way into day 13**

---
⚠️ **TRAP**

A shrinking crew does not always finish. The most a crew of $n$ shrinking by one per day can ever deliver is $n(n+1)/2$ worker-days. Check that this exceeds the total work before you start summing.

**Example 34** `Microsoft · pattern`

Pipe A alone fills a tank in 12 minutes and pipe B alone in 18 minutes. Both are opened at the start, but A is shut off some minutes before the tank is full. The tank is full exactly 12 minutes after the start. For how many minutes before the end was A shut off?

**Solution**

**Insight: B runs the whole time, so compute B's contribution first. Whatever is left is A's, and A's running time follows at once.**

B runs for the full 12 minutes:
$ 12 \times 1/18 = 12/18 = 2/3 " of the tank" $

So A must supply
$ 1 - 2/3 = 1/3 " of the tank" $

A fills at $1/12$ of the tank per minute, so A's running time is
$ (1/3) \div (1/12) = 1/3 \times 12 = 4 " minutes" $

A ran for the first 4 minutes of the 12, so it was shut off
$ 12 - 4 = 8 " minutes before the end" $

Check: $4 \times 1/12 = 1/3$ and $12 \times 1/18 = 2/3$, and $1/3 + 2/3 = 1$. Correct.

**➜ Answer: 8 minutes before the end**

**Example 35** `Uber · pattern`

Six men or 9 women can finish a survey in 20 days.
(a) How long will 4 men and 12 women take?
(b) If the team in (a) is paid Rs.~90,000 in total, and pay follows work done, what does each man and each woman receive?

**Solution**

**Insight: convert everything into one currency of labour. Here, express every woman as a fraction of a man.**

**(a)** Six men and 9 women do the same job in the same time, so
$ 6 "men" = 9 "women" \quad \\Rightarrow \quad 1 "man" = 9/6 = 3/2 "women" \quad \\Rightarrow \quad 1 "woman" = 2/3 "man" $

Total work $= 6 "men" \times 20 "days" = 120$ man-days.

The new team, in man-equivalents:
$ 4 + 12 \times 2/3 = 4 + 8 = 12 " men" $
$ "days" = 120/12 = 10 " days" $

**(b)** Over those 10 days:

Men contribute $4 \times 10 = 40$ man-days. \
Women contribute $8 \times 10 = 80$ man-days (using their man-equivalent of 8). \
Total $= 120$ man-days, as expected.

Men's share $= 40/120 \times 90000 = 1/3 \times 90000 = $ Rs.~30,000, split between 4 men:
$ 30000/4 = 7500 $
so each man gets Rs.~7,500.

Women's share $= 80/120 \times 90000 = 2/3 \times 90000 = $ Rs.~60,000, split between 12 women:
$ 60000/12 = 5000 $
so each woman gets Rs.~5,000.

Check: $30000 + 60000 = 90000$, and a man earns $7500/5000 = 3/2$ times a woman, matching their efficiency ratio.

**➜ Answer: (a) 10 days   (b) Rs.~7,500 per man, Rs.~5,000 per woman**

**Example 36** `Adobe · pattern`

Two men and 3 boys finish a job in 10 days. Three men and 2 boys finish the same job in 8 days. In how many days will 1 man and 1 boy finish it?

**Solution**

**Insight: the question asks for $m + b$, not for $m$ and $b$ separately. Adding the two equations gives $5m + 5b$ in one step, so never solve the system.**

Let $m$ and $b$ be the daily work of one man and one boy.
$ 10 (2m + 3b) = 1 \quad \\Rightarrow \quad 2m + 3b = 1/10 $
$ 8 (3m + 2b) = 1 \quad \\Rightarrow \quad 3m + 2b = 1/8 $

Add them:
$ 5m + 5b = 1/10 + 1/8 = 4/40 + 5/40 = 9/40 $
$ m + b = 9/200 $
$ "days" = 200/9 \approx 22.22 " days" $

Verification by solving fully. From $2m + 3b = 0.1$ and $3m + 2b = 0.125$: \
multiply the first by 3 and the second by 2 to get $6m + 9b = 0.3$ and $6m + 4b = 0.25$; \
subtract: $5b = 0.05$, so $b = 0.01$; then $2m = 0.1 - 0.03 = 0.07$, so $m = 0.035$. \
$m + b = 0.045 = 9/200$. Same answer.

**➜ Answer: $200/9 \approx 22.22$ days**

**Example 37** `Amazon · pattern`

A can finish a job in 12 days and B in 20 days. A works alone for the first $k$ days and then hands over to B, who finishes it. The whole job must be delivered within 16 days. Find the smallest whole number of days $k$ that A must work.

**Solution**

**Insight: write the total elapsed time as a single linear function of $k$. Every extra day A works removes more than one day from the schedule, because A is faster.**

After $k$ days A has done $k/12$ of the job. \
B must do the rest, $1 - k/12$, at $1/20$ per day, so B works
$ 20 (1 - k/12) = 20 - (20 k)/12 = 20 - (5 k)/3 " days" $

Total elapsed time:
$ T = k + 20 - (5k)/3 = 20 - (2k)/3 $

Each day A works shortens the schedule by $2/3$ of a day. Require $T \\le 16$:
$ 20 - (2k)/3 \\le 16 \quad \\Rightarrow \quad (2k)/3 \\ge 4 \quad \\Rightarrow \quad k \\ge 6 $

Smallest whole number: $k = 6$.

Check: A works 6 days and does $6/12 = 1/2$. B must do $1/2$, which takes $20 \times 1/2 = 10$ days. \
Total $= 6 + 10 = 16$ days, exactly on the deadline.

**➜ Answer: $k = 6$ days**

**Example 38** `Google · pattern`

Three taps fill a tank in 12, 15 and 30 hours. They are opened one at a time for exactly one hour each, in rotation, starting with the fastest.
(a) How long does the tank take to fill?
(b) Show that the answer changes if the rotation starts with the slowest instead.

**Solution**

**Insight: a full 3-hour cycle always delivers the same amount whatever the order. Order only decides how the final partial cycle is filled, and the fastest tap clears the remainder quickest.**

LCM of 12, 15 and 30 is 60, so let the tank be 60 units. \
Tap A: $60/12 = 5$ units per hour. \
Tap B: $60/15 = 4$ units per hour. \
Tap C: $60/30 = 2$ units per hour. \
One cycle of 3 hours $= 5 + 4 + 2 = 11$ units.

**(a) Order A, B, C.** \
Five cycles $= 15$ hours and $5 \times 11 = 55$ units. \
Remaining $= 60 - 55 = 5$ units. \
Hour 16 belongs to A, who delivers exactly 5 units. Tank full.
$ "time" = 16 " hours" $

**(b) Order C, B, A.** \
Five cycles $= 15$ hours and 55 units, exactly as before. Remaining $= 5$ units. \
Hour 16 belongs to C: 2 units, running total 57, remaining 3. \
Hour 17 belongs to B, who fills 4 units per hour but needs only 3, taking
$ 3/4 = 0.75 " of an hour" $
$ "time" = 16 + 0.75 = 16.75 " hours" $

The gap is 45 minutes, created entirely inside the last partial cycle.

**➜ Answer: (a) 16 hours   (b) 16.75 hours with the reversed order**

## 📋 One-page revision card

**The one idea**
Finish in $a$ days $\\Rightarrow$ one day's work is $1/a$. Add rates, never days.
$ "together" = 1/a + 1/b \quad \\Rightarrow \quad "time" = (a b)/(a+b) $

**LCM method**: let total work $=$ LCM of the day counts. Every rate becomes a whole number and all fractions disappear.

**Efficiency**: efficiency $prop 1/"time"$. Times $a : b$ $\\Rightarrow$ efficiencies $b : a$.
"$p$ percent more efficient" $\\Rightarrow$ time $\div (1 + p/100)$.

**Work equation**: $(M_1 D_1 H_1)/W_1 = (M_2 D_2 H_2)/W_2$.
Man-days (or worker-hours) is the safest way to handle crews that change size.

**Wages**: split in the ratio of work actually done. Put all rates over one common denominator and read the numerators as the ratio.

**Three pair-totals**: if the pairs take $p$, $q$, $r$ days, add $1/p + 1/q + 1/r$ and halve it to get the rate of all three; then subtract one pair rate to get the missing person.

**Pipes**: filling positive, emptying negative, net rate $=$ sum.
Fills in $a$, with a leak takes $b$: leak alone empties in $a b/(b - a)$.

**Alternate days**: value one 2-day block, fit whole blocks, then finish day by day. Put the faster worker first to minimise the total.

**Someone leaves $d$ days before the end**: that person worked $T - d$ days, where $T$ is the unknown total. Write one equation in $T$.

**Shrinking crew**: daily output is an AP. Work after $k$ days $= sum$ of the AP. A crew of $n$ losing one per day can never deliver more than $n(n+1)/2$ worker-days.

**Expectation vs teamwork**: picking one worker at random averages the **times**; putting them all on the job averages the **rates**. The second is always far faster.

**Top five traps**
+ Subtracting days instead of rates. B is not $(20 - 12)$ days; B's rate is $1/12 - 1/20$.
+ Giving the outlet pipe a plus sign, or forgetting the leak entirely.
+ Splitting wages in the ratio of the day counts. It must be the inverse ratio.
+ Reading "leaves 5 days before the end" as "worked 5 days".
+ Stopping an alternate-day or rotation problem at a whole day. The last turn is usually a fraction of a day, and the final answer is rarely an integer.
