# Chapter 5 — Profit, Loss & Discount

*Cost price is the base. Marked price is the story.*

## What you need to know

**WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW**

**The three prices.** **CP** = cost price (what the seller paid, all costs in). **SP** = selling price (what the buyer paid). **MP** = marked price / list price (the sticker, before any discount).

**Profit and loss — always measured on CP**

$ "Profit" = "SP" - "CP"   "Profit%" = "Profit"/"CP" \times 100   "Loss%" = "Loss"/"CP" \times 100 $

$ "SP" = "CP" \times (100 + p)/100   "CP" = "SP" \times 100/(100 + p) $

For a loss of $l$%, put $-l$ in place of $p$.

**Discount — always measured on MP**

$ "Discount%" = ("MP" - "SP")/"MP" \times 100   "SP" = "MP" \times (100 - d)/100 $

**Successive discounts.** Multiply the remaining factors: $"SP" = "MP" (1 - d_1/100)(1 - d_2/100)(1 - d_3/100)$. For two discounts the single equivalent is $d = d_1 + d_2 - d_1 d_2/100$.

**Markup and discount together.** Mark up $m$% over CP, then give $d$% discount:

$ "Profit%" = m - d - (m d)/100   "SP"/"CP" = (100+m)/100 \times (100-d)/100   "MP"(100-d) = "CP"(100+p) $

**Same SP, one at $+x$% and one at $-x$%:** always a **loss** of $x^2/100$ per cent.

**Markup $m$% then discount $m$%:** always a **loss** of $m^2/100$ per cent.

**Cost of $a$ articles = SP of $b$ articles** $(a > b)$: Profit% $= (a - b)/b \times 100$.

**False weight (dishonest dealer) selling at cost price,** and the same dealer who also marks up $m$ per cent:

$ "Gain%" = ("true weight" - "weight given")/("weight given") \times 100   "SP"/"CP" = (100+m)/100 \times ("true weight")/("weight given") $

**Markup needed** to keep a profit of $p$% after a discount of $d$%: $"MP"/"CP" = (100+p)/(100-d)$.

**Profit as a share of SP.** If profit is $p$% of CP, it is $p/(100+p) \times 100$ per cent of SP.

**Break-even.** With contribution $c =$ SP per unit $-$ variable cost per unit: break-even units $=$ fixed cost $/ c$, and profit $=$ units $\times c -$ fixed cost.

## Warm-up

### Warm-up

**Example 1**

A shirt costs Rs.~480 and sells for Rs.~552. Find the profit and the profit%.

**Solution**

Profit $= 552 - 480 = 72$, that is Rs.~72.

Profit% $= 72/480 \times 100$.

$72/480 = 0.15$, and $0.15 \times 100 = 15$.

**➜ Answer: Profit Rs.~72, profit 15%**

**Example 2**

A bag costs Rs.~1,250 and is sold at a 12% loss. Find the SP.

**Solution**

$"SP" = 1250 \times (100-12)/100 = 1250 \times 88/100$.

$1250 \times 88 = 110000$. Then $110000 / 100 = 1100$.

**➜ Answer: Rs.~1,100**

**Example 3**

An article is sold for Rs.~621 at a profit of 15%. Find the CP.

**Solution**

$"CP" = 621 \times 100/115$.

$621 \times 100 = 62100$. Then $62100 / 115 = 540$.

Check: $540 \times 1.15 = 621$. Correct.

**➜ Answer: Rs.~540**

**Example 4**

A lamp is marked Rs.~900. A 20% discount is given. Find the SP.

**Solution**

$"SP" = 900 \times 80/100 = 900 \times 0.8 = 720$.

**➜ Answer: Rs.~720**

**Example 5**

MP is Rs.~1,600 and SP is Rs.~1,360. Find the discount%.

**Solution**

Discount $= 1600 - 1360 = 240$.

Discount% $= 240/1600 \times 100 = 0.15 \times 100 = 15$.

**➜ Answer: 15%**

**Example 6**

MP is Rs.~2,000. Discounts of 20% and then 10% are given. Find the SP and the single equivalent discount.

**Solution**

After 20%: $2000 \times 0.8 = 1600$.

After 10% on 1600: $1600 \times 0.9 = 1440$.

Total discount $= 2000 - 1440 = 560$.

Single equivalent $= 560/2000 \times 100 = 28$%.

**➜ Answer: SP Rs.~1,440; single discount 28%**

**Example 7**

CP is Rs.~250. The seller wants a 24% profit. Find the SP.

**Solution**

$"SP" = 250 \times 124/100$.

$250 \times 124 = 31000$. Then $31000/100 = 310$.

**➜ Answer: Rs.~310**

**Example 8**

An item sold for Rs.~460 gives a loss of 8%. Find the CP.

**Solution**

$"CP" = 460 \times 100/92$.

$460 \times 100 = 46000$. Then $46000/92 = 500$.

Check: $500 \times 0.92 = 460$. Correct.

**➜ Answer: Rs.~500**

## Tier 1 — Service companies

### Tier 1 — Service · TCS NQT, Accenture, Infosys, Wipro, Capgemini

**Example 9** `TCS NQT pattern`

A trader buys 24 notebooks for Rs.~1,080 and sells each at Rs.~54. Find the profit%.

**Solution**

CP of one notebook $= 1080/24 = 45$.

Profit on one $= 54 - 45 = 9$.

Profit% $= 9/45 \times 100$.

$9/45 = 0.2$, so profit% $= 20$.

**➜ Answer: 20%**

**Example 10** `Accenture pattern`

A shopkeeper marks his goods 40% above cost and then allows a 25% discount. Find his profit%.

**Solution**

Take $"CP" = 100$ (any number works; 100 is easiest).

$"MP" = 100 + 40 = 140$.

$"SP" = 140 \times (100-25)/100 = 140 \times 0.75 = 105$.

Profit $= 105 - 100 = 5$ on a CP of 100.

Profit% $= 5$%.

**➜ Answer: 5% profit**

---
💡 **SHORTCUT**

**Markup $m$%, discount $d$%, in one line:**
$ "Profit%" = m - d - (m d)/100 $
Here: $40 - 25 - (40 \times 25)/100 = 40 - 25 - 10 = 5$. Same answer, no CP needed.

If the answer comes out negative, it is a loss of that much %.

**Example 11** `Infosys pattern`

By selling an article for Rs.~810 a man loses 10%. At what price must he sell it to gain 20%?

**Solution**

Step 1 — find CP.

$"CP" = 810 \times 100/90$.

$810 \times 100 = 81000$. Then $81000/90 = 900$.

Step 2 — new SP for 20% gain.

$"SP" = 900 \times 120/100 = 900 \times 1.2 = 1080$.

**➜ Answer: Rs.~1,080**

---
⚠️ **TRAP**

Do **not** say "he lost 10%, so now add 30% to Rs.~810". The 10% and the 20% are both on the **CP**, not on the old SP. You must go back to CP first, every time.

**Example 12** `Wipro pattern`

Find the single discount equivalent to three successive discounts of 25%, 20% and 10%.

**Solution**

Work with the price that remains, not the price removed.

Remaining factor $= 0.75 \times 0.80 \times 0.90$.

$0.75 \times 0.80 = 0.60$.

$0.60 \times 0.90 = 0.54$.

So 54% of MP is paid, which means 46% is taken off.

**➜ Answer: 46%**

---
⚠️ **TRAP**

Successive discounts are **never** added. $25 + 20 + 10 = 55$ is wrong; the true figure is 46%. Each later discount is charged on a smaller number.

**Example 13** `Capgemini pattern`

A man sells two watches for Rs.~2,400 each. On one he gains 20%, on the other he loses 20%. Find his overall gain or loss%.

**Solution**

Watch 1 (20% gain): $"CP"_1 = 2400 \times 100/120 = 240000/120 = 2000$.

Watch 2 (20% loss): $"CP"_2 = 2400 \times 100/80 = 240000/80 = 3000$.

Total CP $= 2000 + 3000 = 5000$.

Total SP $= 2400 + 2400 = 4800$.

Loss $= 5000 - 4800 = 200$.

Loss% $= 200/5000 \times 100 = 0.04 \times 100 = 4$.

**➜ Answer: 4% loss**

---
💡 **SHORTCUT**

**Same SP, one at $+x$% and one at $-x$%: always a loss of $x^2/100$ %.**

Here $x = 20$, so loss $= 400/100 = 4$%. It is never a "no profit no loss" situation, because the losing item always had the bigger CP.

**Example 14** `Cognizant pattern`

The cost price of 12 articles equals the selling price of 9 articles. Find the profit%.

**Solution**

Let the CP of one article be Rs.~1.

CP of 12 articles $= 12$.

So SP of 9 articles $= 12$, giving SP of one article $= 12/9 = 4/3$.

Profit on one $= 4/3 - 1 = 1/3$.

Profit% $= (1/3)/1 \times 100 = 100/3 = 33 1/3$ %.

**➜ Answer: $33 1/3$% profit**

---
💡 **SHORTCUT**

CP of $a$ articles = SP of $b$ articles $arrow.r$ Profit% $= (a-b)/b \times 100$.

Here $(12-9)/9 \times 100 = 3/9 \times 100 = 33 1/3$ %. If $a < b$ it is a loss.

**Example 15** `TCS NQT pattern`

A dealer allows a 10% discount on the marked price and still gains 17%. If the cost price is Rs.~900, find the marked price.

**Solution**

Step 1 — SP from CP.

$"SP" = 900 \times 117/100 = 900 \times 1.17 = 1053$.

Step 2 — MP from SP.

SP is 90% of MP, so $"MP" = 1053/0.9$.

$1053 / 0.9 = 10530/9 = 1170$.

Check: $1170 \times 0.9 = 1053$, and $1053 - 900 = 153$; $153/900 = 0.17$. Correct.

**➜ Answer: Rs.~1,170**

**Example 16** `Accenture pattern`

A shopkeeper sells goods at cost price but uses a weight of 900 g in place of 1 kg. Find his gain%.

**Solution**

He charges the price of 1000 g but hands over 900 g.

His real cost is the cost of 900 g. His revenue is the price of 1000 g.

Gain (in grams of value) $= 1000 - 900 = 100$.

Gain% $= 100/900 \times 100 = 10000/900 = 100/9 = 11 1/9$ %.

**➜ Answer: $11 1/9$% (about 11.11%)**

---
⚠️ **TRAP**

The base is the **weight he actually gave**, not 1000 g. Writing $100/1000 \times 100 = 10$% is the most common wrong answer here. He gained on what he parted with, and he parted with 900 g.

**Example 17** `Infosys pattern`

An item is marked at Rs.~4,000. Two successive discounts of 20% and 5% are given. Find the SP and the total discount%.

**Solution**

After 20%: $4000 \times 0.80 = 3200$.

After 5% on 3200: $3200 \times 0.95 = 3040$.

Total discount $= 4000 - 3040 = 960$.

Discount% $= 960/4000 \times 100 = 0.24 \times 100 = 24$.

Check with the two-discount formula: $20 + 5 - (20 \times 5)/100 = 25 - 1 = 24$. Correct.

**➜ Answer: SP Rs.~3,040; total discount 24%**

**Example 18** `Wipro pattern`

A fruit seller buys 100 kg of apples at Rs.~45 per kg. 10% of them rot and are thrown away. At what price per kg must he sell the rest to make a 20% profit on his total cost?

**Solution**

Total CP $= 100 \times 45 = 4500$.

Target SP $= 4500 \times 1.20 = 5400$.

Apples left $= 100 - 10 = 90$ kg.

Price per kg $= 5400/90 = 60$.

**➜ Answer: Rs.~60 per kg**

**Example 19** `Capgemini pattern`

A food stall has a fixed monthly cost of Rs.~36,000. Each meal costs Rs.~70 to make and sells for Rs.~130. (a) How many meals must it sell in a month to break even? (b) What is the profit if it sells 900 meals?

**Solution**

Contribution per meal $= 130 - 70 = 60$.

(a) Break-even meals $= 36000/60 = 600$.

(b) At 900 meals: total contribution $= 900 \times 60 = 54000$.

Profit $= 54000 - 36000 = 18000$.

**➜ Answer: (a) 600 meals   (b) Rs.~18,000**

**Example 20** `Cognizant pattern`

A retailer buys an item listed at Rs.~2,500 at a 20% trade discount. He marks it at the list price and sells it to a customer at a 10% discount on the list price. Find his profit%.

**Solution**

His CP $= 2500 \times 0.80 = 2000$.

His SP $= 2500 \times 0.90 = 2250$.

Profit $= 2250 - 2000 = 250$.

Profit% $= 250/2000 \times 100 = 0.125 \times 100 = 12.5$.

**➜ Answer: 12.5%**

## Tier 2 — Singapore & Thailand

### Tier 2 — Singapore & Thailand · Grab, Shopee, GIC, DBS, Agoda, SCB

**Example 21** `Grab · pattern`

A restaurant lists a meal set at THB~250 on a delivery app. The app keeps an 18% commission on the listed price. The restaurant's own cost per set is THB~160. Find the profit per set and the profit% on cost.

**Solution**

Money the restaurant keeps $= 250 \times (100-18)/100 = 250 \times 0.82$.

$250 \times 0.82 = 205$.

Profit $= 205 - 160 = 45$.

Profit% $= 45/160 \times 100$.

$45/160 = 0.28125$, so profit% $= 28.125$.

**➜ Answer: THB~45 per set; 28.125% profit**

---
⚠️ **TRAP**

A platform commission is a cut of the **listed price**, so it behaves exactly like a discount — it lowers SP. It is not a cost added to CP. If you had written $"CP" = 160 + 45 = 205$ you would get the wrong base.

**Example 22** `Shopee · pattern`

A seller buys 300 phone cases at S$3.20 each and lists them at S$7.50. He sells 180 at the list price. To clear stock he then cuts the price by 40% and sells 100 more. The last 20 are written off as unsold. Find his overall profit%.

**Solution**

Total CP $= 300 \times 3.20 = 960$.

Full-price sales: $180 \times 7.50 = 1350$.

Discounted price $= 7.50 \times 0.60 = 4.50$.

Clearance sales: $100 \times 4.50 = 450$.

Unsold 20: revenue 0.

Total SP $= 1350 + 450 = 1800$.

Profit $= 1800 - 960 = 840$.

Profit% $= 840/960 \times 100$.

$840/960 = 0.875$, so profit% $= 87.5$.

**➜ Answer: 87.5%**

Unsold stock is **not** subtracted from CP. He paid for all 300; he only earned on 280. That is why write-offs hurt twice.

**Example 23** `Sea · pattern`

A store marks its cost up by 80%. In a sale it gives 25% off, and members get a further 10% off the already reduced price. For a member sale, find (a) the profit%, and (b) the total discount% off the marked price.

**Solution**

Take $"CP" = 100$.

$"MP" = 180$.

After 25%: $180 \times 0.75 = 135$.

After a further 10%: $135 \times 0.90 = 121.5$.

(a) Profit $= 121.5 - 100 = 21.5$ on a CP of 100, so profit% $= 21.5$.

(b) Discount off MP $= 180 - 121.5 = 58.5$.

Discount% $= 58.5/180 \times 100$.

$58.5/180 = 0.325$, so 32.5%.

Check with the two-discount rule: $25 + 10 - (25 \times 10)/100 = 35 - 2.5 = 32.5$. Correct.

**➜ Answer: (a) 21.5% profit   (b) 32.5% total discount**

**Example 24** `LINE MAN · pattern`

A grocer in Bangkok buys rice at THB~40 per kg and marks it up 25%. He also uses a 900 g weight whenever he sells "1 kg". Find his actual profit%.

**Solution**

For each "1 kg" sale:

He charges $40 \times 1.25 = 50$, that is THB~50.

He actually hands over 900 g, which cost him $0.9 \times 40 = 36$, that is THB~36.

Profit $= 50 - 36 = 14$.

Profit% $= 14/36 \times 100 = 1400/36 = 350/9 = 38 8/9$ %.

**➜ Answer: $38 8/9$% (about 38.89%)**

---
💡 **SHORTCUT**

**Two cheats multiply, they do not add.**
$ "SP"/"CP" = (100+m)/100 \times ("true weight")/("weight given") $
Here $1.25 \times 1000/900 = 1.25 \times 1.1111 = 1.3889$, so about 38.89% profit. Adding $25 + 11.11 = 36.11$ is wrong.

**Example 25** `GIC · pattern`

A Singapore trader imports 500 gadgets from India at Rs.~1,950 each when S$1 = Rs.~65. Shipping and duty add S$5 per unit. He sells each at S$45.50. Find the profit% and the total profit.

**Solution**

Cost of one gadget in SGD $= 1950/65 = 30$.

Add shipping and duty: $30 + 5 = 35$. This is the CP per unit.

SP per unit $= 45.50$.

Profit per unit $= 45.50 - 35 = 10.50$.

Profit% $= 10.50/35 \times 100$.

$10.50/35 = 0.30$, so profit% $= 30$.

Total profit $= 500 \times 10.50 = 5250$.

**➜ Answer: 30% profit; total S$5,250**

**Example 26** `Agoda · pattern`

A 40-room hotel has a fixed cost of THB~24,000 per night. Each occupied room costs THB~400 in cleaning and utilities, and the room rate is THB~1,200. Find (a) the break-even number of rooms, (b) the result on a night when 28 rooms are sold, (c) the occupancy% needed to break even.

**Solution**

Contribution per room $= 1200 - 400 = 800$.

(a) Break-even rooms $= 24000/800 = 30$.

(b) At 28 rooms: contribution $= 28 \times 800 = 22400$.

$22400 - 24000 = -1600$. That is a loss of THB~1,600.

(c) Occupancy needed $= 30/40 \times 100 = 0.75 \times 100 = 75$ %.

**➜ Answer: (a) 30 rooms   (b) loss of THB~1,600   (c) 75% occupancy**

Below break-even every empty room still carries its share of the fixed cost. That is why hotels discount hard once the night is nearly over — any price above THB~400 helps.

**Example 27** `DBS · pattern`

A wholesaler sells to a retailer at a 25% profit, and the retailer sells to a customer at a 20% profit. The customer pays Rs.~4,500. Find the wholesaler's cost price.

**Solution**

Work backwards, one layer at a time.

Retailer's CP $= 4500/1.20$.

$4500/1.2 = 45000/12 = 3750$. This is also the wholesaler's SP.

Wholesaler's CP $= 3750/1.25$.

$3750/1.25 = 375000/125 = 3000$.

Check forward: $3000 \times 1.25 = 3750$; $3750 \times 1.2 = 4500$. Correct.

**➜ Answer: Rs.~3,000**

**Example 28** `SCB · pattern`

By what percentage above cost must a seller mark his goods so that after allowing a 20% discount he still earns a 30% profit?

**Solution**

Let $"CP" = 100$ and let the markup be $m$%.

$"MP" = 100 + m$.

$"SP" = ("MP") \times 0.80$, and this must equal $130$.

So $("MP") \times 0.80 = 130$.

$"MP" = 130/0.80 = 13000/80 = 162.5$.

Markup $= 162.5 - 100 = 62.5$.

**➜ Answer: 62.5% above cost**

---
💡 **SHORTCUT**

**Markup needed** $= ((100+p)/(100-d) - 1) \times 100$.

Here $130/80 = 1.625$, so 62.5%. Memorise the shape: **wanted profit factor divided by remaining-after-discount factor.**

**Example 29** `LINE MAN · pattern`

A cloud kitchen buys 500 kg of chicken at THB~85 per kg. A freezer failure spoils 8% of it. The usable chicken is turned into meals — 1 kg makes 4 meals. All other costs (rent, staff, other ingredients) come to THB~77,500. Each meal sells for THB~75 and all meals are sold. Find the profit%.

**Solution**

Chicken cost $= 500 \times 85 = 42500$.

Total cost $= 42500 + 77500 = 120000$.

Usable chicken $= 500 \times (100-8)/100 = 500 \times 0.92 = 460$ kg.

Meals made $= 460 \times 4 = 1840$.

Revenue $= 1840 \times 75$.

$1840 \times 75 = 1840 \times 70 + 1840 \times 5 = 128800 + 9200 = 138000$.

Profit $= 138000 - 120000 = 18000$.

Profit% $= 18000/120000 \times 100 = 0.15 \times 100 = 15$.

**➜ Answer: 15%**

**Example 30** `Grab · pattern`

Three stores sell the same jacket, all marked S$480. Store A gives 30% off then a further 10% off. Store B gives 20% off then a further 20% off. Store C gives a flat 37% off. Rank the three final prices.

**Solution**

**Store A:** factor $= 0.70 \times 0.90 = 0.63$.

$480 \times 0.63 = 302.40$.

**Store B:** factor $= 0.80 \times 0.80 = 0.64$.

$480 \times 0.64 = 307.20$.

**Store C:** factor $= 1 - 0.37 = 0.63$.

$480 \times 0.63 = 302.40$.

A and C are exactly equal at S$302.40. B is dearer by $307.20 - 302.40 = "S$"4.80$.

**➜ Answer: A = C = S$302.40 (cheapest); B = S$307.20**

---
⚠️ **TRAP**

"30% then 10%" **is** exactly 37%, not 40%. And "20% then 20%" is 36%, not 40%. Equal-looking discount pairs are not equal: for a fixed total $d_1 + d_2$, the pair that is **more lopsided** takes off more. Check: $30+10$ gives 37% off, $20+20$ gives 36% off.

**Example 31** `Goldman Sachs · pattern`

Two articles are sold at the same price, one at a profit of $x$% and one at a loss of $x$%. Prove the net result is always a loss of $x^2/100$ %. Then find $x$ if the net loss is 9%.

**Solution**

**Insight: the two cost prices are not equal. The loss-making article had the larger CP, so the loss is computed on a bigger base than the profit.**

Let each selling price be $S$.

$ "CP"_1 = (100 S)/(100+x)   "CP"_2 = (100 S)/(100-x) $

Total CP $= 100 S [ 1/(100+x) + 1/(100-x) ]$.

Add the fractions: $ 1/(100+x) + 1/(100-x) = ((100-x) + (100+x))/((100+x)(100-x)) = 200/(10000 - x^2) $

So total CP $= (20000 S)/(10000 - x^2)$.

Total SP $= 2 S$.

Loss $= (20000 S)/(10000-x^2) - 2S = (20000 S - 2S(10000 - x^2))/(10000 - x^2) = (2 S x^2)/(10000 - x^2)$.

The numerator has $x^2 > 0$, so the loss is positive whatever $x$ is. It is always a loss.

Loss% $= "Loss"/"Total CP" \times 100 = (2 S x^2)/(10000-x^2) \times (10000-x^2)/(20000 S) \times 100$.

The bracket $(10000-x^2)$ and $S$ both cancel:

$ "Loss%" = (2 x^2)/20000 \times 100 = x^2/100 $

Now set $x^2/100 = 9$, so $x^2 = 900$ and $x = 30$.

**➜ Answer: Net loss is always $x^2/100$ %; a 9% net loss means $x = 30$**

**Example 32** `Amazon · pattern`

A dealer cheats twice. When buying, his weight is set so that he receives 1,100 g for every 1,000 g he pays for. When selling, he gives only 900 g for every 1,000 g he charges for. He quotes exactly his cost price. Find his overall gain%.

**Solution**

**Insight: stop counting rupees and count grams. Each cheat is a clean multiplying factor on the grams that move.**

Let the honest market rate be $c$ per gram.

**Buying.** He pays for 1000 g, i.e. he pays $1000c$, and gets 1100 g.

His true cost per gram $= (1000 c)/1100 = (10 c)/11$.

**Selling.** He charges the price of 1000 g, i.e. $1000 c$, and gives 900 g.

His revenue per gram delivered $= (1000 c)/900 = (10 c)/9$.

**Profit per gram delivered** $= (10c)/9 - (10c)/11 = 10c (1/9 - 1/11) = 10 c \times (11-9)/99 = (20 c)/99$.

Gain% $= ((20c)/99) / ((10c)/11) \times 100 = (20c)/99 \times 11/(10 c) \times 100$.

$= 220/990 \times 100 = 2/9 \times 100 = 22 2/9$ %.

**One-line check with factors:** $ "SP"/"CP" = 1100/1000 \times 1000/900 = 1.1 \times 1.111... = 1.2222... $ which is a gain of $22 2/9$ %.

**➜ Answer: $22 2/9$% (about 22.22%)**

**Example 33** `Google · pattern`

A store sells 200 units a week at S$50 each. Each unit costs S$30. Market tests show that for every S$1 cut in price, weekly sales rise by 20 units. What price maximises weekly profit, and what is that profit?

**Solution**

**Insight: profit is a quadratic in the size of the price cut. Write it out and take the vertex — do not guess prices one by one.**

Let the price be cut by $n$ dollars.

Price $= 50 - n$.

Quantity $= 200 + 20 n$.

Margin per unit $= (50 - n) - 30 = 20 - n$.

Weekly profit
$ P(n) = (20 - n)(200 + 20 n) $

Expand:
$ P(n) = 20 \times 200 + 20 \times 20n - 200 n - 20 n^2 = 4000 + 400 n - 200 n - 20 n^2 $
$ P(n) = 4000 + 200 n - 20 n^2 $

This is a downward parabola. Its peak is at $n = -b/(2a)$ with $a = -20$ and $b = 200$:

$ n = -200/(2 \times (-20)) = 200/40 = 5 $

So cut S$5.

Price $= 50 - 5 = 45$. Quantity $= 200 + 100 = 300$. Margin $= 15$.

Profit $= 300 times 15 = 4500$.

**Sanity check on either side:**

$n = 4$: margin 16, quantity 280, profit $= 4480$.

$n = 6$: margin 14, quantity 320, profit $= 4480$.

Both are below 4500, so $n = 5$ is the peak. (At $n = 0$ the profit was only $200 times 20 = 4000$.)

**➜ Answer: Sell at S$45; maximum weekly profit S$4,500**

---
💡 **SHORTCUT**

**The midpoint rule for linear demand.** Let $k$ be the extra units sold per 1 unit of price cut. Define the **choke price** — the price at which sales would fall to zero:
$ "choke price" = "current price" + "current quantity"/k $
Then the profit-maximising price is the **midpoint of the cost price and the choke price**:
$ "best price" = ("cost" + "choke price")/2 $
Here $k = 20$, so choke $= 50 + 200/20 = 60$, and best price $= (30 + 60)/2 = 45$. Same answer in one line.

**Example 34** `D. E. Shaw · pattern`

A shopkeeper has 100 identical items. Each costs him Rs.~90, and he also has a fixed cost of Rs.~1,500 for the batch. He will sell every item, each at either Rs.~150 (full price) or Rs.~120 (clearance). He wants a profit of at least 25% on his total cost. What is the smallest number he must sell at full price?

**Solution**

**Insight: start from the worst case — everything at Rs.~120 — and note that each upgrade to full price adds exactly Rs.~30. The problem becomes a single division.**

Total cost $= 100 \times 90 + 1500 = 9000 + 1500 = 10500$.

Required SP $= 10500 \times 1.25$.

$10500 \times 1.25 = 10500 + 2625 = 13125$.

Let $k$ items sell at Rs.~150 and $(100-k)$ at Rs.~120.

$ "SP" = 150 k + 120(100 - k) = 150k + 12000 - 120k = 12000 + 30 k $

Need $12000 + 30k \\ge 13125$.

$30 k \\ge 1125$.

$k \\ge 1125/30 = 37.5$.

$k$ must be a whole number, so $k = 38$.

**Verify both sides.**

$k = 38$: SP $= 12000 + 1140 = 13140$. Profit $= 13140 - 10500 = 2640$.
$2640/10500 = 0.2514...$, i.e. 25.14%, which clears 25%.

$k = 37$: SP $= 12000 + 1110 = 13110$. Profit $= 2610$.
$2610/10500 = 0.2486...$, i.e. 24.86%, which fails.

**➜ Answer: 38 items at full price**

**Example 35** `Microsoft · pattern`

A seller buys one unit for Rs.~500. With probability 0.6 it sells in week 1 at Rs.~800. If it does not sell, he must cut the price by 25% in week 2, and at that price it sells with probability 0.75. If it still does not sell, it is scrapped for Rs.~100. Find his expected profit and expected profit%.

**Solution**

**Insight: a discount decision is judged on expected revenue, not on the headline price. Build the tree and weigh every leaf.**

Week-2 price $= 800 \times 0.75 = 600$.

**Leaf 1:** sells in week 1. Probability 0.6, revenue 800.

**Leaf 2:** fails week 1, sells week 2. Probability $0.4 \times 0.75 = 0.30$, revenue 600.

**Leaf 3:** fails both. Probability $0.4 \times 0.25 = 0.10$, revenue 100.

Probabilities add to $0.6 + 0.3 + 0.1 = 1$. Good.

Expected revenue:
$ E = 0.6 \times 800 + 0.30 \times 600 + 0.10 \times 100 $
$ E = 480 + 180 + 10 = 670 $

Expected profit $= 670 - 500 = 170$.

Expected profit% $= 170/500 \times 100 = 0.34 \times 100 = 34$.

**➜ Answer: Expected profit Rs.~170, i.e. 34% on cost**

Compare: if he refused ever to discount, revenue would be $0.6 \times 800 + 0.4 \times 100 = 480 + 40 = 520$, a profit of only Rs.~20. The 25% discount is worth Rs.~150 in expectation.

**Example 36** `Adobe · pattern`

A shopkeeper marks his goods up by $m$% and then gives a discount of $m$% on the marked price. Show that he always loses, and find $m$ if his loss is 4%.

**Solution**

**Insight: the markup is taken on CP and the discount on the bigger MP, so they cannot cancel. Algebraically it is a difference of squares.**

Take $"CP" = 100$.

$"MP" = 100(1 + m/100)$.

$"SP" = "MP" \times (1 - m/100) = 100(1 + m/100)(1 - m/100)$.

Use $(1+a)(1-a) = 1 - a^2$ with $a = m/100$:

$ "SP" = 100(1 - m^2/10000) = 100 - m^2/100 $

Since $m^2/100 > 0$ for any $m \\ne 0$, SP is always below 100. He always loses.

Loss $= m^2/100$ on a CP of 100, so

$ "Loss%" = m^2/100 $

Set $m^2/100 = 4$, giving $m^2 = 400$ and $m = 20$.

**Check with $m = 20$:** CP 100, MP 120, SP $= 120 \times 0.8 = 96$. Loss 4 on 100 = 4%. Correct.

**➜ Answer: Always a loss of $m^2/100$ %; a 4% loss means $m = 20$**

**Example 37** `Uber · pattern`

A dealer sells at cost price but uses a faulty balance. On a nominal 1 kg sale his gain is 25%. He now sells in nominal 5 kg lots with the same balance, but wants his gain on such a lot to be exactly 10%. What percentage discount must he give on his quoted price?

**Solution**

**Insight: the faulty balance is a fixed multiplier on revenue. Find that multiplier once, then choose the discount that pulls the product down to 1.10.**

**Step 1 — how heavy is his "1 kg"?**

Let the weight he actually gives be $w$ grams.

$ "Gain%" = (1000 - w)/w \times 100 = 25 $

$ 1000 - w = 0.25 w $

$ 1000 = 1.25 w $

$ w = 1000/1.25 = 800 " g" $

**Step 2 — the 5 kg lot.**

Nominal 5 kg means he delivers $5 \times 800 = 4000$ g.

Let the honest cost be $c$ per gram.

His cost $= 4000 c$.

His quoted price (cost price of 5000 g) $= 5000 c$.

**Step 3 — the discount.**

Let the discount be $d$%. Revenue $= 5000 c (1 - d/100)$.

For a 10% gain: revenue $= 1.10 \times 4000 c = 4400 c$.

$ 5000 c (1 - d/100) = 4400 c $

Cancel $c$:

$ 1 - d/100 = 4400/5000 = 0.88 $

$ d/100 = 0.12 $

$ d = 12 $

**Check:** $5000 \times 0.88 = 4400$ against a cost of 4000, a gain of $400/4000 = 10$%. Correct.

**➜ Answer: A 12% discount**

**Example 38** `Goldman Sachs · pattern`

A trader has exactly Rs.~9,000 and must spend all of it. Item A costs Rs.~300 and sells at a 20% profit. Item B costs Rs.~200 and sells at a 35% profit. He must buy at least 10 of each, and only whole items. What mix gives the largest total profit, and how much is it?

**Solution**

**Insight: with a fixed budget, rank the items by profit per rupee spent, not by profit per item. Item A earns more per item but less per rupee.**

Profit per item:

A: $300 \times 0.20 = 60$.

B: $200 \times 0.35 = 70$.

Profit per rupee spent:

A: $60/300 = 0.20$.

B: $70/200 = 0.35$.

B is better per rupee, so push money into B and cut A to its floor of 10.

**Budget equation.** Let $a$ be the number of A and $b$ the number of B.

$ 300 a + 200 b = 9000 $

Divide by 100:

$ 3 a + 2 b = 90 $

For $b$ to be a whole number, $90 - 3a$ must be even, so $3a$ must be even, so $a$ must be even.

Smallest even $a$ with $a \\ge 10$ is $a = 10$.

$ 2 b = 90 - 30 = 60   b = 30 $

Check $b = 30 \\ge 10$. Valid.

Profit $= 10 \times 60 + 30 \times 70 = 600 + 2100 = 2700$.

**Compare the next options to be sure.**

$a = 12$: $2b = 90 - 36 = 54$, $b = 27$. Profit $= 720 + 1890 = 2610$.

$a = 14$: $2b = 90 - 42 = 48$, $b = 24$. Profit $= 840 + 1680 = 2520$.

Profit falls as $a$ rises, exactly as the per-rupee ranking predicted.

Overall profit% $= 2700/9000 \times 100 = 30$ %.

**➜ Answer: 10 of A and 30 of B; maximum profit Rs.~2,700 (30% on the Rs.~9,000)**

## 📋 One-page revision card

**The bases — get these right and half the traps die**

- Profit% and loss% are on **CP**.
- Discount% is on **MP**.
- Commission and platform fees behave like discounts: they cut SP.

**Core formulas**

$ "Profit%" = ("SP"-"CP")/"CP" \times 100   "SP" = "CP" (100+p)/100   "CP" = (100 "SP")/(100+p) $

$ "SP" = "MP" (100-d)/100   "MP"(100-d) = "CP"(100+p) $

**Shortcuts worth memorising**

- Markup $m$%, discount $d$%: Profit% $= m - d - (m d)/100$.
- Two discounts: single $= d_1 + d_2 - (d_1 d_2)/100$.
- Three or more discounts: multiply the remaining factors, e.g. $0.75 \times 0.80 \times 0.90 = 0.54$, so 46% off.
- Same SP, $+x$% and $-x$%: always a **loss** of $x^2/100$ %.
- Markup $m$% then discount $m$%: always a **loss** of $m^2/100$ %.
- CP of $a$ articles = SP of $b$ articles: Profit% $= (a-b)/b \times 100$.
- False weight, selling at CP: Gain% $= ("true" - "given")/"given" \times 100$.
- False weight **and** markup: multiply the factors, $"SP"/"CP" = ((100+m)/100) \times ("true"/"given")$.
- Markup needed for profit $p$% after discount $d$%: $"MP"/"CP" = (100+p)/(100-d)$.
- Profit is $p$% of CP $arrow.r$ it is $p/(100+p) \times 100$ % of SP.
- Break-even units $= "fixed cost" / ("SP" - "variable cost")$.
- Linear demand: best price $=$ (cost $+$ choke price)$/2$, where choke price $=$ current price $+$ current quantity$/k$ and $k$ is the extra units sold per 1 unit of price cut. (Or expand the quadratic and take its vertex.)

**The top 5 traps**

+ **Adding successive discounts.** 30% then 10% is 37% off, not 40%.
+ **Using the wrong base.** A 10% loss then a 20% gain are both on CP — return to CP before moving on. Never build a new percentage on an old SP.
+ **False weight base.** Gain is on the weight **given**, not on 1000 g. 900 g for a kilo is $11 1/9$% gain, not 10%.
+ **Forgetting fixed costs.** A batch's CP is item cost $\times$ quantity **plus** fixed cost. Unsold stock is still paid for; it lowers SP, it does not lower CP.
+ **Assuming equal-and-opposite cancels.** $+x$% and $-x$% on equal SPs is a loss. Markup $m$% then discount $m$% is a loss. Different bases never cancel.
